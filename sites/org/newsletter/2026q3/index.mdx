## 🤔 Why did you join FreeSewing?

I've been working on our website a bit (see below) and as I was fixing some
positioning issues on the _Reasons to join_ section, I realized that all these
reasons are written from our vantage point. But people probably have
their own reasons, and those are just as valid, if not more so.

So, we've added space for an additional reason that is randomly picked from
a list of reasons submitted by our users. Obviously, that makes most sense
when we have a diverse list of reasons, so that's where you come in.

We'd like to hear why you decided to join FreeSewing, or in retrospect, why do
you think it’s a good idea for others to do so?

You can share your input [on our
forum](https://forum.freesewing.eu/t/why-did-you-join-freesewing/1124), or just
hit reply, that works too.

## 👕 Ashley, Hannah, Polly, Tina and Toni: Our five latest designs

Over the last 3 months, we've seen 3 minor releases of FreeSewing: versions 4.8, 4.9, and 4.10.
Together, they combine for 5 new designs:

- [Ashley A-Line Shorts](https://freesewing.eu/designs/ashley/) by Gawain:
  > _Ashley extends the Titan block. Much of its code is directly lifted from
  > Percy, for cutting the legs short, drafting the pockets, and automatically
  > setting a curve for the waistband (though Ashley adds a more dramatic curve
  > to the back waistband only instead of curving all the waistband pieces).
  > The fly pieces and belt loops are pilfered from Charlie. The big new thing
  > Ashley does is add a yoke to the back pieces for some extra shaping at your
  > butt. This is a common detail on jeans._
- [Hannah Hoodie](https://freesewing.eu/designs/hannah/) by Jonathan:
  > _Hannah is a relatively fitted hoodie pattern with optional support for
  > fitting curvy bodies and larger busts, bums and bellies and different
  > sleeve style options._ > _Hannah is based on Toni Top and the hood from Hugo and Yuri._
- [Polly Plush](https://freesewing.eu/designs/polly/) by Gawain:
  > _Polly is a base to make simple humanoid or anthropomorphic fabric dolls.
  > It has many options to change the size and proportions of the doll, and a
  > couple of alternate heads and legs to allow for more animal-like
  > characters_
- [Toni Top](https://freesewing.eu/designs/toni/) by Jonathan:
  > _Toni is a configurable t-shirt or top design that supports different
  > sleeve styles. It has basic support for fitting large busts, bellies and
  > bums. These special adjustments are only enabled if you provide the related
  > optional measurements._
- [Tina Top](https://freesewing.eu/designs/tina/) by Jonathan:
  > _Tina is a simple overlap/faux wrap top based on the Toni top._

## 🏗️ Infrastructure as (open source) code

If sewing or sewing pattern is all you care about, then I guess you can stop
reading here, for the remainder of this newsletter will be dedicated to the
questions raised in our previous edition, and in particular, what steps we've
taken in the mean time.

As a reminder, in our previous edition of this newsletter, I talked about being
spread too thin, and how we wanted to grow the project horizontally to ensure
that we cannot merely cope, but continue to thrive, offer more designs, a
better service, and so on.

As part of that endeavor, in May [we have open-sourced FreeSewing's
infrastructure](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/src/branch/develop/ansible).
The configuration of various servers and the services running
on them, how the systems are set up, Ansible playbooks to manage them, and so
on.

At a time when other people and organizations are actively closing off
repositories because of the threat of vulnerability discoveries by AI models,
this is perhaps counter-intuitive, if not ill-advised.

However, our aim here is clear: We want to get more people actively involved.
So this is why we are dragging more of what goes on behind the scenes into the
sunlight, in the hopes that it attracts contributors who have an interest in
that behind-the-scenes stuff.

If you are one of those people who are interested in learning by doing, then
please keep reading, there's more to come.

## 🎨 The ongoing rewrite of FreeSewing.eu

Another area where we need to find more help is in maintaining our website. To
facilitate that, we have embarked on a refactor to port our website from
Docusaurus/webpack to Vite in an effort to slash build times and memory
consumption. This should make it easier for people to dip their toes in, since
currently even getting the website's development environment to run is
something that simply won't work for some contributors due to memory
constraints.

We're not there yet, but we've got most of the functionality up and running,
and the results are rather satisfying.

On the build infrastructure, which gives us a good comparison for total build time (prebuild + build), here's the speed-up:

- `develop` branch (Docusaurus): 814 seconds (13:34)
- `eusite` branch (Vite): 44 seconds

There's one big catch though: We (currently) do not generate SSR output for the vite site.
This is something we might add later, although these days I'm not certain that
it's all that important. I mean, it's important for SEO but to what extent is
that relevant in the age of LLMs. Even people who use Google are not seeing
traditional search results.

As I mentioned, this is a work-in-progress. There's an [open pull
request](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/pulls/871) for it, and
perhaps more importantly, we publish preview builds at
[preview.freesewing.eu](https://preview.freesewing.eu/).

So feel free to check it out and kick the tires, even though we know there are
still sharp edges, we appreciate the feedback. Oh, and some of you will be
happy to hear that we've brought back themes, including the _hax0r_ theme,
which was a fan favorite.

## 🤝 The FreeSewing Fellowship

There is, in the academic, professional, or vocational contexts, this concept
of a _fellowship_. It's basically an exchange where one party wants to learn,
and the other could use some help. And so they make a deal that benefits them
both. For example, if you wanted to learn to be a blacksmith, you would try to
find a blacksmith who would take you under their wing and learn the trade, in
return for you helping out.

In the same way, we would like to launch a _FreeSewing fellowship_. The idea
being that if you are somebody -- or know somebody -- who is interested in what we
do, then please step forward. We will teach you everything we know in
exchange for your help.

Here's some food for thought:

- This FreeSewing newsletter goes out to over 63K subscribers. Have you ever
  sent an email to 63K people, or tried to? How would you do that? And how
  much would it cost?
- Our backend database holds just over 520K user accounts. Are you involved in
  a project that has half a million user accounts? What database would you use?
  How would you structure it?
- Our main website consumes about 7 to 9 terabytes of traffic every month. Does
  that seem like a lot? Where would you host it? And what sort of bill can you
  expect for that amount of traffic?
- The editor on our preview site is currently broken due to an issue that is
  known as _the temporal dead zone_ in JavaScript. Have you ever heard
  of it? Does it sound kinda intriguing? Or rather boring?
- Our editor is called as a single React component, but under the hood there's
  plenty of _prop-drilling_ which tends to trigger frequent re-renders.
  Would a React context be better, or should we switch to something like
  Zustand for state management? Maybe we should be glad it works at all and not
  touch it?

There are no right or wrong answers to these questions. Chances are, these are
not even questions that you find particularly interesting. And that's fine.
You're here for the sewing patterns, right?

I am including these questions for the people who do find (some of) them
interesting. Because those are the people we are hoping will want to consider
our fellowship.

To be clear, it would be great to have people step forward who know the answers
to all of these questions. But that is probably not very realistic.

So instead, we're looking for people who do not know (all) the answers, but do
find the questions interesting, and would like to learn more.

> _Fellowships that are unpaid are typically seen as a *bad thing* because it's
> understood that only people with wealth (or wealthy parents) can undertake such a
> fellowship._
>
> _FreeSewing does not have the financial means to offer a paid fellowship.
> But, we also do not expect the kind of time commitment of a (full-time) job.
> Rather, this is something you would do on the side, as a hobby or learning track._

In summary, this is the deal: We'll teach you everything we know, in return for your help.

- If you're interested, please hit reply and let us know.
- If you know somebody who could be interested, feel free to forward this open invitation.

Thank you for considering it.
